Screened first

Feeds are untrusted input. A dedicated security model inspects every article for prompt injection and content engineered to manipulate downstream AI -- and it is deliberately conservative: when in doubt, it flags. Anything that fails is recorded with its reasoning and kept out of curation entirely.

Curated, not steered

A separate model scores what survives screening against your keywords and thresholds. It judges relevance, not category, so nothing is buried for being off-trend or boosted for being divisive. You set the bar; Herald ranks to it.

Stories, not duplicates

The same event from a dozen sources collapses into one cluster. Herald groups related articles across feeds with vector embeddings, so you read the story once instead of scrolling past ten copies of it.

How it works

  1. Subscribe. Import your existing subscriptions from OPML, or add feeds one at a time. RSS and Atom both work.
  2. Screen. Each fetched article is checked for manipulation before it goes any further. Unsafe content is quarantined, not curated.
  3. Score. Cleared articles are ranked against your interest keywords and a threshold you control.
  4. Cluster. Related coverage is grouped across sources so you see one story, not ten.
  5. Read anywhere. Browse in the web reader, or sync via OPML and the Fever API into the RSS client you already use.

Why two models instead of one

Most AI news tools either skip security -- leaving them open to poisoned feeds -- or fold safety and editorial judgment into a single model, where the two fight each other. A safety-trained model curates defensively and buries anything it finds sensitive; a relevance-tuned model has no defenses at all.

Herald splits the jobs at the architecture level. One model is responsible only for whether content is safe. A different one is responsible only for whether it is interesting to you. Neither has to compromise, so you get real protection and neutral ranking at the same time.
